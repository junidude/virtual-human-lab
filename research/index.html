Research

We study how to read a person’s biology and reason about it. Three projects, one long-term goal: a model of how an individual responds.

01 · Bulk RNA → single cells

PBISC

Generate a single-cell view from one bulk blood sample.

Watch the model
Question

Can a bulk RNA profile of blood, which is cheap and widely collected, give a useful single-cell view?

Approach

A diffusion model generates cell-level counts from bulk PBMC RNA-seq, with no matched single-cell reference at use time.

Status

Manuscript under review at BIBM.

02 · RNA → one token

RNA-LLM

Let a language model read a whole transcriptome as one token.

Question

Can a language model reason over RNA without reading thousands of gene values as text?

Approach

A learned bridge compresses a single-cell, pseudobulk or bulk profile into one token the model reads.

Status

Manuscript under review at BIBM.

03 · Clinical reasoning

RL-env

Environments where AI agents work up a patient case, step by step.

Question

Can an AI agent learn to work up a case the way a clinician does, and be scored fairly for it?

Approach

The agent takes a history, examines and orders tests, then commits to a decision. It is scored on accuracy, test cost and safety.

Status

Early work. First tasks: triage after a positive antinuclear-antibody (ANA) test, and diagnosis tasks built from published case reports. Synthetic cases only.

Lead: Seung Jun Lee

How we test our models

New people, new studies

Models are scored on donors and studies they never saw in training.

Limits stated up front

Each result says what it cannot show and when not to use it.

Records and corrections

Data sources, methods and dated corrections stay on record. Research practice

None of this is a clinical tool. VHL does not predict individual treatment outcomes or give medical advice.
