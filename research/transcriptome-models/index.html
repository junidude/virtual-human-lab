Transcriptome Models

When a model learns to read gene expression, does it also get better at biology? We compared six single-cell models and four bulk RNA models on both kinds of test.

Compare the rankings

Choose a task and a gene expression test. Select a model to follow it across the two rankings.

On Tabula Sapiens, the six models ranked in the same order for gene detection and cell-type classification. On PANACEA, all four bulk models scored lower on drug response after pretraining.

      Two patterns to explore

      The same six places

      On Tabula Sapiens, every model held the same rank for gene detection and cell-type classification. The order changed when we ranked the models by how much they gained from pretraining.

      A lower score after pretraining

      On PANACEA, all four bulk RNA models scored lower on drug-response prediction after pretraining. Each was compared with its own untrained version.

      Which model led on RNA-based questions?

      We also tested how much probability the six single-cell models assigned to reference answers about the identity of a cell or sample. We evaluated one training run per model.

      How the scores were calculated

      The model names describe different ways of training an RNA encoder. Within each RNA modality, the models trained on the same corpus. Each score is the mean of three runs. For pretraining gain, we first subtract the matching untrained score in each run, then average the three differences. Both versions use a fitted readout for the task.

      Ranks compare models within one task. The left and right tests can use different units. These rankings describe the models tested here. Small differences in rank can correspond to nearly identical scores.
